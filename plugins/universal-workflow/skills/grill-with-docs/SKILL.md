# Grill with Docs

1. Read the proposed plan and available evidence, including any prior research. Resolve factual questions from sources, artifacts, or inspection. Identify the decisions the user still owns and the dependencies between them.
2. Interview in rounds. Ask the consequential questions whose prerequisites are settled, starting with those that unlock later choices. For each, recommend an answer and explain its trade-off. Probe vague terms or disputed rules with a concrete scenario. Send the round and wait for answers before asking dependent questions.
3. Record decisions as they settle. Use the project's existing brief, plan, decision log, or glossary where appropriate. Otherwise, keep concise notes with the plan in its requested format. Record terms whose meanings affect the work. Give a durable decision its own record when it is costly to reverse, its rationale will matter later, and it involved a real trade-off. Label open questions separately from confirmed decisions.
4. Summarize the agreed outcome, scope, constraints, and observable criteria for success. Include any open issue with its consequence and who can resolve it. Ask the user to confirm the plan or explicitly authorize proceeding with the remaining assumptions. End the interview when the plan is confirmed and any remaining issues have an agreed disposition.
